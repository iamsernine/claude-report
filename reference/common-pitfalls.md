# Common pitfalls

Ordered roughly by how often they cost marks.

## Structural

1. **Inverted proportions.** Company presentation and état de l'art swallow the
   report; the contribution gets twelve pages. See `length-and-proportions.md`.
2. **A state of the art with no positioning.** Listing existing technologies is
   not a literature review. The chapter must end with a comparative analysis
   placing *your* work against those solutions. Reports that skip this get
   hammered.
3. **An introduction that gives away the results.** The introduction sets up the
   problem; it does not report findings.
4. **A conclusion introducing new material.** The conclusion answers the
   questions raised in the introduction. Nothing new appears there. Put openings
   in a separate "Perspectives" sub-section.
5. **Chapters that do not hand off.** Without a closing paragraph linking to the
   next chapter, the report reads as disconnected documents stapled together.
6. **Using Plan A for a research project.** A UML class diagram chapter in a
   machine-learning PFE is filler. Use the research plan instead.

## Content

7. **Unreproducible methodology.** The methodology must be complete enough that a
   competent reader could reproduce the approach. The validity of the report
   depends on it. Missing: hyperparameters, data split, random seeds, hardware,
   library versions.
8. **Uninterpreted results.** Every result must be interpreted, including the
   ones that contradict expectations — that is frequently where the originality
   lies.
9. **No baseline.** A model scoring 0.91 means nothing without something to
   compare against. Report a trivial baseline and a prior-work baseline.
10. **Metrics chosen after seeing the results.** Define the evaluation protocol
    before the results chapter, and say so.
11. **Uncritical description of the existing system.** "Critique de l'existant"
    means critique, not inventory.
12. **Difficulties hidden.** Naming what went wrong and what limited the results
    reads as maturity, not weakness. Juries ask about it anyway.

## Formal

13. **Illustrations never referenced in the text.** Every figure and table must
    be cited and justified where it appears.
14. **Inconsistent citation style.** Pick numbered or author–date and never mix.
15. **Screenshots at unreadable resolution.** The single most common cosmetic
    failure in IT reports.
16. **Plagiarism.** Total or partial reproduction of work that is not your own is
    treated as a disciplinary offence. Paraphrase and cite; quote sparingly and
    mark it.
17. **Untranslated or absent English abstract.** Costs nothing, adds visibility.
18. **A table of contents generated by hand.** Use Word heading styles or LaTeX
    so it stays correct after the last edit.

## Process

19. **Writing the report in the last two weeks.** Draft the context and état de
    l'art chapters while the implementation is still running.
20. **No reference manager.** Reformatting sixty references by hand the night
    before submission is avoidable.
21. **Missing the deposit deadline or its conditions.** Number of copies, digital
    copy in both PDF and editable format, signed forms. A late deposit is
    penalised and can be refused.
