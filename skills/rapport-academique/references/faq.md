# FAQ

Questions students actually ask that the outlines do not answer.

**How many references is normal?**
Roughly 25–40 for a PFE, 15–25 for a PFA, 5–15 for a stage technicien, and very
few for a stage d'initiation. Research-flavoured PFEs sit at the top of that
range or above. A PFE with six references signals a thin état de l'art; one with
120 signals padding.

**Does an application project still need an état de l'art?**
Yes, but a short comparative one — three to six pages reviewing existing products
or approaches and explaining why none of them fits. It is not a literature review
of academic papers. What is mandatory in every case is the positioning: the
comparison table and the gap.

**Can I write in English?**
Usually yes, and increasingly common in Moroccan engineering schools, but the
résumé in French is generally required regardless. Confirm with your department
before committing — discovering this two weeks before submission is expensive.

**Can I reuse figures from papers or documentation?**
For explanatory figures, yes, with the source in the caption. Never for results —
a results figure must be yours. Redrawing a borrowed diagram in your own style,
still cited, is better than screenshotting a PDF.

**What if my results are negative?**
Report them. A negative result correctly obtained and correctly interpreted is a
legitimate contribution, and juries respect it. What is not acceptable is
adjusting the protocol until the number improves and not saying so. Every result
must be interpreted, including the ones that contradict expectations.

**My subject changed halfway through the internship. What do I write?**
Write the report on the final subject, and mention the reorientation in the
methodology section and the difficulties section. Do not try to make it look
like the plan was always this. Juries have seen it a hundred times; they care how
you handled it.

**How long should the company presentation be?**
Shorter than you think. Two to four pages for a PFE. It is context, not subject.
A ten-page company presentation in a PFE is the most visible symptom of inverted
proportions.

**Do I need a Gantt chart?**
Almost always expected in French-tradition reports. Generate it from real dates.
If your project ran differently from the plan, show both — planned and actual —
and explain the gap. That is more interesting than a tidy fiction.

**Should I include code in the report?**
Short excerpts only, and only where they illustrate a design decision. Never as
filler. Full listings go in the annexes, or better, a repository link. If you
include code, it must be syntax-highlighted and readable at print size.

**How many figures?**
Roughly one per two to three pages of body text as a rough target. Below that the
report reads as a wall of prose; far above it, as a slide deck. Every one must be
referenced in the text.

**Can I write "je" or must I write "nous"?**
Both are accepted. `nous` is the safer default and is required for group work.
Whichever you pick, hold it consistently — mixed voice is a visible flaw.

**Should I put my report on GitHub?**
Check with your host organisation first. Internship reports frequently contain
information the company considers confidential — architecture, client names,
internal metrics. Ask before publishing, and get it in writing.

**What if I have no results yet and the deadline is close?**
Draft everything that does not depend on results — context, état de l'art,
methodology, experimental protocol. These are roughly 60% of the document and
they do not change when the numbers arrive. This is precisely what the
placeholder system is for.

**Is using an AI assistant for this allowed?**
Depends entirely on your institution, and policies are changing fast. Ask. What
is universally true: the structure, the drafting scaffolding, and the formatting
are tooling; the problématique, the engineering decisions, the results, and the
interpretation must be yours. A report you cannot defend in front of a jury is
worthless regardless of how it was produced.
