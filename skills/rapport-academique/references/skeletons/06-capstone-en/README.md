# 06 — IT capstone report (English)

**Use this when:** you are writing in English — an international programme, a
double-degree submission, an exchange, or a report intended for a non-francophone
reader.

This is not a translation of the French plan. Anglophone IT capstone conventions
differ in three ways that matter:

1. **The abstract does real work.** It is read as a standalone artefact and is
   often the only thing a reviewer reads in full.
2. **A user manual and a project legacy section are expected.** French plans
   rarely include them; they are standard here, and they are genuinely useful.
3. **Chapter naming follows the problem, not the process.** "Problem Analysis"
   rather than "Contexte général du projet".

## Capstone vs. thesis

Worth being clear about which you are writing, because the expectations differ.
A **thesis** generates original knowledge through original research, carries a
much higher expectation of background research and justification, and is often
expected to result in a paper submitted somewhere. A **capstone** applies
existing knowledge to a practical problem and produces recommendations or a
deliverable.

If you are on the thesis side, use `02-pfe-research-ml` and translate the
headings — that structure is closer to what a thesis committee expects.

## Shape

| Chapter | Answers |
|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | What problem, why does it matter, what is the scope? |
| 2. Literature Review / Existing Systems | What has been done? |
| 3. Technical Background | What technologies are involved? |
| 4. Methodology | How was it approached? |
| 5. Design and Implementation | What was built? |
| 6. Testing and Evaluation | Does it work? |
| 7. Conclusion and Future Work | What now? |

Merge 2 and 3 if the project is not research-heavy.
