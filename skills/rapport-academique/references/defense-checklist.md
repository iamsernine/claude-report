# Defense (soutenance) checklist

The written report is only part of the grade. A representative split:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| The work itself (effort, rigour, supervisor's assessment) | 30% |
| Quality of the written document | 30% |
| Quality of the oral presentation | 20% |
| Quality of the discussion with the jury | 20% |

Half the grade is the document and the slides, not the engineering.

## Format

- 15–20 minutes of presentation, followed by jury questions.
- Jury of three: a president, an examiner (preferably in the subject's
  discipline), and your supervisor. A guest may attend.

## Slides

- About **fifteen slides for fifteen minutes**. Budget 1–2 minutes per slide.
- Maximum ~25 words per slide — title plus keywords.
- **One major idea per slide.**
- Minimum 18 pt text. One consistent template throughout.
- Slide 1: title, authors, institution, supervisors, jury, date.
- Slide 2: the plan.
- Then: context and objectives → method → results → conclusion.
- Avoid dense tables. Prefer diagrams, screenshots, short demo video.

## What a defense is not

A defense presents the **marrow** of the project — it moves away from detail
toward synthesis. It is not a summary of the written document and it is not an
attempt to say everything. Give the jury the desire to know more; those who want
detail will read the report.

## During the questions

- Take notes while jury members speak. Do not interrupt.
- Rather than answering each comment one by one, **group the remarks by theme**
  (methodology, results, choices) and answer synthetically. This demonstrates a
  capacity for synthesis that juries reward.
- If you do not know, say so. Attempting an answer that drags you into unfamiliar
  territory is worse than a clean "I don't know, but here is how I would find out."
- Criticism during a defense is normal. Stay calm and courteous.

## Questions to prepare in advance

Jury members reliably ask about:

- Points that were asserted but not justified.
- Anything left undeveloped in the report.
- Perspectives and possible extensions.
- Why you chose this technology over the obvious alternative.
- What you would do differently with three more months.
- For AI/data work: how you split the data, whether there is leakage, what the
  baseline was, and why the metric you chose is the right one.

## Rehearsal

Rehearse aloud and timed, at least three times. Do not read from a script and do
not memorise it — a memory lapse in a recited talk is unrecoverable. Cards are
acceptable as a safety net, but the presentation must not be a reading of them,
and eye contact with the jury has to be maintained.
