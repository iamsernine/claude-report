# Length and proportions

This is the rule set that most reports violate, and it is the easiest place to
lose marks for reasons that have nothing to do with engineering quality.

## The proportion rule

| Block | Share |
|---|---|
| Introduction | ~1/5 |
| Development (the chapters) | ~3/5 |
| Conclusion | ~1/5 |

More than **three quarters** of the report should be devoted to results,
development, interpretation and discussion. Use the annexes to protect that
ratio.

## Hard caps that recur across guides

| Element | Cap |
|---|---|
| General introduction | 1–2 pages, never more |
| General conclusion | 2 pages |
| Abstract | 150–250 words |
| Keywords | 5–7 |
| Annexes | ≤ 15 pages for a 60-page report |
| Whole report (excl. annexes) | 30 pages in the strictest guides |

The 30-page ceiling is department-specific — Moroccan engineering PFEs commonly
run 50–80 pages. The **proportions** are what generalise, not the absolute count.

## The classic failure mode

Twenty-five pages of company presentation and state of the art, followed by
twelve pages of actual contribution. The jury reads the ratio before it reads
the content. If your état de l'art is longer than your results chapter, cut it.

## Things that belong in the annexes, not the body

- Long code listings
- Raw data tables and full result dumps
- Questionnaires, interview grids, meeting minutes
- Full API specifications
- Installation and configuration procedures
- Detailed UML diagrams that a reader does not need in order to follow the argument

## Chapter-level habits

- Open each chapter with a short introduction stating what it will establish.
- Close each chapter with a conclusion that hands off explicitly to the next one
  ("...which makes it possible to move to the design phase, the subject of the
  following chapter").
- **Write the general introduction last**, once you actually master the subject
  and your reasoning has been laid out. The introduction must never state the
  results.
